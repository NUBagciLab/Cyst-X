Cyst-X is a public multicenter MRI benchmark for intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN) risk stratification: 1,461 T1-weighted and T2-weighted MRI volumes from 764 patients at seven centers, released with expert pancreas masks, risk labels, patient-level splits and reference segmentation and classification models.

Abstract

Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMNs) are pancreatic cancer precursors, and guideline-based risk stratification both over-treats indolent lesions and misses progression. We present Cyst-X, a public benchmark of 1,461 T1-weighted (T1W) and T2-weighted (T2W) MRI volumes from 764 patients at seven centers, with expert pancreas masks, risk labels, splits and reference AI risk-prediction models. PanSegNet segmented the pancreas with Dice of 86.34% (T1W) and 89.26% (T2W) on held-out centers. For the primary task, high-risk versus low-risk IPMN, a 3D DenseNet-121 achieved cross-validated AUCs of 74.59% and 73.45%; complementary broad binary and three-class tasks reached 78.67–81.28% and 73.47–75.76%. Because patient images often cannot be pooled across institutions, we also simulated federated learning across the seven centers. On T2W MRI, federated FedProx was similar to centralized training in center-averaged external AUC (71.00% versus 71.18%) but not in pooled AUC (62.35% versus 77.49%). Radiologist comparisons showed cohort-dependent sensitivity–specificity tradeoffs. Cyst-X supports reproducible method development and identifies within-IPMN risk discrimination and cross-center score comparability as open challenges.

Dataset Composition

  • Multicenter cohort: 1,461 MRI volumes (723 T1W, 738 T2W) from 764 patients at seven centers: New York University Langone Health (NYU), Mayo Clinic Florida (MCF), Northwestern University (NU), Allegheny Health Network (AHN), Mayo Clinic Arizona (MCA), Istanbul University Faculty of Medicine (IU) and Erasmus Medical Center (EMC). Scanners span GE, Siemens and Philips at 1.5 T and 3 T.
  • High-risk: IPMN with high-grade dysplasia or worse, including carcinoma in situ and invasive carcinoma, confirmed by endoscopic-ultrasound-guided biopsy or surgical histopathology.
  • Low-risk: IPMN with low- or intermediate-grade dysplasia confirmed on resection or tissue sampling, or IPMN without biopsy that remained stable over at least three years of imaging follow-up (growth below 2.5 mm per year, no worrisome features or high-risk stigmata under the Kyoto criteria).
  • No-risk: normal pancreatic imaging without cystic lesions, or benign cysts not associated with IPMN, as determined on imaging.

IPMN Risk Classification Benchmark

The primary task separates high-risk from low-risk IPMN. Two complementary tasks are reported: broad binary (high-risk IPMN versus low-risk IPMN plus no-risk) and three-class classification. Models are evaluated by patient-level five-fold cross-validation and by leave-one-center-out external evaluation, with centralized and federated (FedAvg, FedProx) training.

Comparison with Radiologists

Three blinded abdominal radiologists scored the same patients under imaging-only conditions. The comparison is reported as sensitivity and specificity in three cohorts: 629 broad cases, 474 IPMN-only cases and 357 histopathology-confirmed IPMN cases.
